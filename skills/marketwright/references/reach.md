# Procedure: communities, press, creators, devlogs, post calendar

Output: `reach-<slug>.md` with the places, the drafts and the calendar. The user posts and sends; you draft. Read [../kb/ethics.md](../kb/ethics.md) and [../kb/data.md](../kb/data.md).

## Communities

1. `MKW communities --genre <genre> --general`. Keep the places where the game's players gather and devs are welcome; drop any whose self-promotion is banned or where AI content is rejected when the game uses it.
2. For each kept place: its rule (with the check date), the format that fits (flair, weekly thread such as Screenshot Saturday, feedback thread), and what the user does there besides posting (answer questions, give feedback). Tell the user to read the rules page on the day; mark rows not read live.
3. Each post draft says who made it ("I'm the dev"), shows the game as it plays, asks a real question or shares something useful (a lesson, a GIF of a fix), and fits that place. No cross-posting the same text everywhere.
4. Be a regular there before you need it: answer questions, point at other devs' work (Kleon ch. 7).

## The game's own community (Discord, subreddit, Steam forum)

1. Set it up before linking it: written value and rules, a read-first channel, a few seeded threads (Bacon ch. 7; Vogl ch. 2). Never inflate member counts or claims (Bacon ch. 7).
2. Welcome each newcomer personally. Keep one or two rituals the dev can sustain, such as a weekly devlog thread or a monthly playtest night, with no streaks or guilt prompts (Vogl ch. 3-4).
3. Roles (playtester, helper, moderator) are earned by contribution and never sold. A paid supporter tier is labelled as paid. Pick moderators who want the group to thrive, not the loudest players (Vogl ch. 8, 10).
4. Health is who answers newcomers and who comes back, not the member count (Bacon ch. 8-9). Write down a conflict and appeal procedure, set server hours, and say publicly when the dev is away (Bacon ch. 11).
5. Answer complaints and negative reviews with the specific fix and name it in the patch notes; never offer anything for a changed review (textbook ch. 3).

## Press kit

The contents list in kb/data.md. Hosted page: sitewright, or presskit.html. Include AI use and Early Access status plainly.

## Creators and press

1. Build the list from who covers the closest games (their recent videos or articles), not from follower counts. Each row: name, channel or outlet, the similar game they covered and when, contact route from their own page, language.
2. Keys: Steam keys or Curator Connect, no condition on the verdict, logged; vet requesters (fake journalists ask for keys).
3. Email draft: their name, the similar game they covered, the hook in one line, one GIF or trailer link, the key offer with no strings, embargo date if any, one line asking them to disclose the free copy. Short; never a template blast. The subject line carries the game's one true differentiator; any proof it cites is real and checkable. Pitch what their audience gets, not what the coverage means to you; if you cannot say why their viewers would care, skip them (Doan chs. 8-9).
   Plan keys and festival slots months ahead, picked by whom the target players follow (Wesley and Barczak, Conclusion).
4. Tools: Keymailer, Lurkit (kb/data.md, with their status), Steam Curator Connect.

## Devlogs and the post calendar

1. Cadence the user can keep. One home for long posts (Steam news, itch devlog, a blog), short clips on one or two social platforms the user likes. Posting gets a time box (say 30 minutes) so it never eats the build.
2. What to post at each stage, so a week with nothing new still has a post: early, the influences and the idea; mid-build, methods, tools and work in progress; near launch, cut content and lessons; after launch, what players do with the game. Have the user capture clips, screenshots and a line of notes as they build, so posts are picked rather than made.
3. Stock and flow: short posts keep people aware; once a month gather the best ones into a longer post on a page the user owns (sitewright), which search keeps finding after the platforms change. Add a mailing list sign-up there with a stated cadence; only people who opted in, never imported contacts.
4. Calendar table: date, place, format, draft, asset needed, done. Tie peaks to the plan's dates (announcement, playtest, demo, Next Fest, launch).
5. The devlog audience is the early adopters, not the market: the wider market waits for reviews and creators, so do not judge the launch by devlog reach alone (textbook chs. 1-2).
6. Ethics check: save, run `MKW ethics reach-<slug>.md`, fix every flag.
