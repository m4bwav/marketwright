# Ethics: rules, law, backlash cases and the honest versions

Not legal advice. Sources at the end, checked 2026-11-02. `MKW ethics <file>` scans a plan for the patterns below; judgment still reads every step.

## The question

Would this embarrass the developer if the community saw how it was done? If yes, it is out, whatever the rules say. If it only works while hidden, it is out.

Never decide a deal (sponsor, bundle, key reseller, paid placement) while the need for money outranks what players would accept; assume it will come to light (Overdeliver ch. 9).

## Hard lines and why

| Tactic | Rule or law | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Rewards for reviews, review swaps | Steam bans it [E1]; FTC 16 CFR 465 bans rewards conditioned on a verdict and fake reviews, with civil penalties (in force 2024-10-21) [E3]; UK DMCC Act bans fake and concealed incentivised reviews from 2025-04-06, fines up to 10 percent of turnover [E4] | reviews removed, store trust, legal risk |
| Review prompts inside the game | Steam bans it [E1] | |
| Sock puppets, posing as a fan, agency astroturfing | Reddit spam and manipulation rules; communities expose it (Trap Plan and War Robots: Frontiers, 2025; Cargo 3 and Harmonix, 2015) [E6, E9] | bans, the story outlives the game |
| Bought followers, views, wishlists, votes | FTC 465 bans fake social indicators [E3] | corrupts the numbers you plan with |
| Trailers or ads that show what the game is not | ASA banned Playrix ads in 2020 (a disclaimer did not help) and an Evony ad that hid the core game [E7]; Aliens: Colonial Marines, $1.25M settlement offer (2014) [E8] | refunds, rulings, reputation |
| Undisclosed paid or gifted coverage | FTC Endorsement Guides (2023) and Disclosures 101; ASA/CAP: label "#Ad", not "Sponsored" or "Gifted" (guidance updated October 2026) [E5] | the creator's and your credibility |
| Hidden AI use | Steam content survey [steam.md]; Clair Obscur lost Indie Game Awards wins after AI use it had denied (2025) [E10] | the denial is the scandal |
| Fake scarcity, fake countdowns | EU UCPD and UK unfair practice rules (specific rulings not researched, UNVERIFIED) | |
| Grey-market key sites | G2A dispute, 2019; Valve warns of fake journalists [E1, E11] | chargebacks, lost sales |

## Grey zones and their honest versions

- **Friends and family reviews**: allowed on Steam without a reward; US law bars undisclosed insider reviews. Only real buyers, who say they know the developer, in their own words.
- **Keys to creators**: allowed. No condition on the verdict; ask them to disclose; log every key; send in small batches; vet requesters.
- **Same clip in many communities**: spam rules target it. Two or three communities where it fits, each one's rules and flair, answer comments, space it out.
- **Reddit's "10 percent rule"**: retired site-wide in May 2017 and left to each subreddit; many still apply it. Be a member first: comment and help more than you post.
- **Discounts and founder editions**: fine when real. True prices, a real end date that holds, Steam buyers get the same deal as key buyers.
- **AI-made assets**: disclose on Steam, the store page and the press kit; never deny it to awards or press. Expect some communities (many art and pixel art spaces) to reject AI art outright: do not post it there.
- **Building hype**: promise what the game is. The gap between promise and game turns into negative reviews and refunds; accurate expectations beat high ones (the textbook's ch. 3 claims the opposite; rejected).
- **Shortages and launch stock**: report real limits (keys, playtest slots) truthfully; never invent them to drive urgency.
- **Early footage**: fine labelled "work in progress" and shown as it plays.
- **Asking for wishlists**: fine in your own posts, news and trailers' end cards; never as a swap.
