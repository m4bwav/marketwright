# Reading: Indie Game Marketing Secrets (Mathur, Doan, Hayner, 2017)

Black Shell Media, Kindle, 2 April 2017 (ASIN B06Y1B17L2). Its metadata names Raghav Mathur, Daniel Doan and Raquel Hayner as authors. About 9,000 words, 11 chapters. Read 2026-11-03 with readwright from a copy of Mark's download (`D:/RawStorage/BigDownloads/Unorganized/Indie Game Marketing Secrets .../`). Paraphrased below; nothing is quoted at length.

**Verdict:** thin and dated. It is a studio's marketing brochure more than a field guide: no data and no sources, and the platform advice is from 2016 (140-character tweets, Vine, Steam's 2016 launch impressions, "a dollar a day" Facebook boosts). A few practices hold up and agree with the kb. Nothing in it changes a rule. Status in the beat's literature: `distilled`.

## Kept (folded into references/reach.md)

- **The subject line carries the one real differentiator** (ch. "How to Effectively Email Journalists and YouTubers"). Their example: a "game X launches Friday" subject line did worse than one quoting a real, documented reaction to the game. Kept with the ethics line attached: the hook or proof must be true and checkable. Never invent a quote or imply an endorsement.
- **Research the creator before writing**: a few minutes of their recent videos or articles, and what made the games they covered catch on (same chapter). This matches reach.md step 1.
- **The writer is not doing you a favour** (ch. "Getting Covered by Large Publications"): pitch what their readers get, not what the coverage means to you. If you cannot honestly say why their audience would care, do not pitch that outlet.
- **Each milestone is a beat** (ch. "Generating Buzz by Marketing in Waves"): announcement, alpha or playtest, Early Access, launch and each major update get their own push. This matches the calendar step that ties peaks to the plan's dates.
- **Write each post for its platform** (ch. "How to Fit Marketing into Your Workflow"): the same post copied across networks looks lazy. This matches reach.md's "no cross-posting the same text".
- **Pick a voice, person or studio, and keep it** (ch. "Finding the Right Voice"): a solo dev's name can carry the game, and a studio voice should still sound like the people behind it.

## Rejected or flagged

- **Trend-jacking unrelated hashtags** (ch. "Encouraging Players to Share"): the book advises riding any trending tag even when it has nothing to do with the game. That is hashtag spam on most platforms and fails the "would the community respect it" test. Not taken.
- **Building relationships in order to get coverage** (LinkedIn endorsements, tagging writers in posts): the book does say the friendship has to be real. Endorsing and tagging people to get on their radar is a grey zone. The kb's version: take part in their work honestly, or just pitch them well.
- **Reddit "no more than 10% self-promotional posts"**: the book gives this as Reddit's rule. It was an old site-wide guideline, and each subreddit's own rules now decide. Not taken as a number; the kb's per-community rules stand.
- **Launch checklist items**: "post on r/gaming" (that subreddit's own rules apply), "giveaway on Twitter" (allowed when it follows the platform's promotion rules and the laws on sweepstakes). Kept only as checklist ideas, each checked against the place's rules.
- **All numbers** (ad budgets, tweet lifetime, internet share, Steam impressions): 2016 to 2017 and unsourced. None enter the kb.
- **Publisher context**: Black Shell Media was a marketing and publishing firm selling services to the developers it advised. Read the book with that in mind. What happened to the firm later was not checked here (open question; never state it without a dated source).
