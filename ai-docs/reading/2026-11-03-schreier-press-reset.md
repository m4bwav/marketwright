Jason Schreier, *Press Reset: Ruin and Recovery in the Video Game Industry*, Grand Central Publishing, 2021. Commercial copyright, all rights reserved. EPUB, about 97,900 words: an introduction, nine chapters of reporting, an epilogue. Read 2026-11-03 with readwright from a copy of Mark's download (`D:/RawStorage/BigDownloads/Unorganized/Press Reset Ruin and Recovery in the Video Game Industry by Jason Schreier.epub`). Read selectively: the introduction (start), the epilogue, ch. 8 "Gungeon Keeper" from Mythic's closure to the end (Dodge Roll and Enter the Gungeon), greps in ch. 3 "Rafting Upstream" (The Molasses Flood, The Flame in the Flood) and ch. 9 "Human Costs; Human Solutions" (contract studios, unions). Chs. 1-2 and 4-7 (Irrational, Epic Mickey, 38 Studios, Big Huge Games, Visceral, Bloody Socks) not read beyond grep counts. Paraphrased below; nothing is quoted beyond a short phrase.

**Verdict:** low yield for marketing, as expected. Reporting on studio closures and layoffs at big publishers; its value to the plugin is context on risk and two indie case studies. Nothing in it conflicts with the ethics line; ch. 8 is a ready backlash case against predatory monetisation. Numbers are 2017-2021 period data, not benchmarks. Status in the beat's literature: `skimmed`.

## Kept

- **Industry volatility is the norm** (Introduction: an IGDA 2017 survey found full-time developers averaged about two employers in five years; GamesIndustry.biz counted over a thousand jobs lost to closures in one year to September 2018; Epilogue: more than a dozen closures 2018-2021, Telltale shut with no severance). Context for why many solo and small indie developers are former studio staff, and why they plan around running out of money.
- **Where laid-off developers go** (Epilogue; ch. 9): some start indie studios on savings or severance (Dodge Roll from Mythic, ch. 8; The Molasses Flood from Irrational, ch. 3), some go to small contract or co-development studios (Disbelief, ch. 9, a model its founders expect to last), some leave games for steadier software jobs (a QA tester, Epilogue). Useful for creator and peer outreach: ex-AAA developers are a large, networked indie cohort.
- **Small, own-funded, then a publisher for the business side** (ch. 8). Dodge Roll lived off savings, wanted a publisher for marketing, PR and legal rather than funding, skipped Kickstarter as a full-time job of its own (a footnote), and got a publisher by showing a playable demo in person at E3.
- **Streamers who fit the genre, with early access** (ch. 8). Before launch, Dodge Roll gave early access to streamers of a similar game (The Binding of Isaac) who wanted something new; the streams drew tens of thousands of viewers and preorders. The honest version the plugin already teaches: pick creators who play the genre, no condition on the verdict.
- **Free updates and ports extend the tail** (ch. 8: each free Enter the Gungeon update brought press and a sales spike, more so than the launch window suggested; ch. 3: The Flame in the Flood kept selling past launch and did well on Switch). Indie sales do not follow the AAA launch-then-drop curve; content updates and new platforms are marketing beats.
- **Crowdfunding costs** (ch. 3, footnote): platform and payment fees plus physical backer rewards eat into the total. Context for anyone planning a Kickstarter.
- **Predatory monetisation backfires** (ch. 8, EA's Dungeon Keeper mobile: timers to push gem purchases, which the developers themselves distrusted; internet rage; the studio closed in 2014; Dodge Roll later mocked it with a joke in-game gun). A backlash case for the ethics kb.
- **Self-imposed crunch at a tiny studio** (ch. 8: revenue share and not wanting to let friends down drove sixteen-hour days in a shared flat; the founders were "not friends" by launch). A note for launch plans: schedule the marketing work so the plan does not assume crunch.

## Rejected or flagged

- **Unionisation and labour policy** (ch. 9): important, but not marketing; not taken.
- **AAA studio histories** (chs. 1-2, 4-7): closures caused by executives, licences and investors; no transfer to a solo developer's marketing.
- **Period numbers** (2014-2021 sales, Kickstarter totals, Switch launch-era demand): not current benchmarks; none enter the kb. The "Switch ports sell" point belongs to the console's early years.
- **Telltale's oversaturation** (Epilogue: the same formula on many licences, fans tired): a design and strategy lesson more than marketing; noted only.
